\section{Token-Level Norm Statistics}
\label{app:per-token-norms}

The mean $\|H_K\|$ in Table~\ref{tab:main} aggregates over tokens and can mask per-token outliers, which is a real concern given that real LayerNorm and RMSNorm operate per token.
Table~\ref{tab:per-token} reports the per-token $\|H_K\|_2$ distribution on $20{,}480$ validation tokens for each 2$\times$2 condition (single seed; the across-seed pattern is the same).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\caption{\textbf{Per-loop RMSNorm drift is a token-level heavy-tail failure, not just a mean shift.} Per-token final-loop norm distribution (Euclidean $\|H_K\|_2$) on 20,480 WikiText-103 validation tokens. RMSNorm-readout drift is concentrated in a heavy tail of outlier tokens, especially under per-loop CE: the median is $12$--$21\times$ smaller than the mean, with $1\%$ of tokens reaching $10^5{+}$. Raw readouts produce tighter bounded distributions in every condition.}

The two RMSNorm-readout failure modes are qualitatively different.
Under terminal-only CE, RMSNorm drift is uniform: median $\approx$ mean and the p99 is within $2\times$ of the median.
Under per-loop CE, RMSNorm drift is heavy-tailed: the median is a few thousand but the mean is $12$--$21\times$ larger and a small fraction of tokens reach $10^5{+}$.
Both raw conditions stay tight at every percentile.
