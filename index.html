Where Loops Stop Paying

A looped transformer reuses one block many times, buying compute without buying parameters. Gains saturate after a handful of loops, and the field attributes that to the loop map converging. Here the map never converges and the gains saturate anyway. Three measurements say what actually binds.

Cross-entropy falls steeply to loop 8, reaches 3.6599 at loop 10, and climbs from there. Every integer depth from 1 to 64 was evaluated, so the band edges are located to one loop rather than to the nearest point on a coarse grid.

The model still beats its own single-loop score at loop 64 (3.7673 against 3.9622), so depth past the band stays harmless. It stops being worth anything after 17.

The annealed arm holds 19 and ends at 30 — 2.1× the width, for zero added parameters. It is a schedule over which loop indices receive gradient, so nothing is added to the model.

The same two edges move the same way at 2.5M and 10M tokens, and the result survives halving the tolerance that defines the band.

What this does not buy. The loss half of the claim was withdrawn. Across six paired arms the CE difference reads −0.0811, −0.0609, +0.0482, −0.0902, −0.0764, +0.1119 — a mean of −0.0247, inside the 0.0541 replicate floor. Annealing buys depth, and the released checkpoint is the dense control.

Distinct key projections buy depth diversity, and training spends it

Dose–response measured Causal test undecided
At initialisation After training, 4 projections

A token produces 32 key vectors, one per loop. Anything that attends over depth, or picks a depth to exit at, has to tell them apart. In a weight-tied loop they span an effective rank of 1.603 out of 32 — one shared projection applied to a collinear stream.

Giving the block more key projections raises that almost linearly: 3.097 at two, 5.742 at four, 10.646 at eight, 30.863 at thirty-two. Rank is purchasable, at roughly 1.6 per distinct projection.

Then it was purchased. A trained arm with four projections — +10.0% of the parameter budget — starts at 8.818 and finishes at 1.74, against a tied control's 1.66. This objective drives the representation back to a collapsed state even where the architecture can avoid it.

What stays open. That arm was built to decide whether the rank collapse causes the depth-mixing failure. Its gate, registered before the run, required a trained rank above ~4; it returned 1.74, so the causal question is undecided. One seed, 3.5M tokens. Separately, the rank at initialisation is flat across hidden sizes 224 to 896 — 2.749, 2.687, 2.747, 2.731, 2.718 over a 12× parameter range — so the figure is not an artifact of this model's size.

Four interventions bank their gain at a single loop

Four independent arms

Splitting each intervention's improvement into the part already present at one loop and the part that needs depth: the first part dominates. At r = 1 a depth gate mixes a single state, a cycled adapter applies one branch, and duo-causal attention has no previous loop to read — each mechanism is inert there, and the gain is already banked.

These arms improve the block. The looping keeps whatever it was worth before.

Each row carries a caveat. The depth gate at 96% is an instrument failure — it saturates to a hard argmax and mixes 1.01–1.05 loops of r. Exclusive self attention is two seeds at one budget. Loop-cycled LoRA reverses at scale: −0.0936 at 2.5M tokens against +0.0077 at 12M in a config-identical pair, which leaves the project with no replicated loss improvement at scale. Duo-causal attention lowers CE while its own registered mechanism check reports no engagement.

A fifth intervention runs the other way. The norm penalty wins perplexity outright — 37.52 against 38.86 — by damaging loop 1 (ΔCE@1 = +0.2263) and narrowing the band. The released checkpoint is the arm without that trade.

Every figure is drawn from the stored evaluation arrays, at the precision the report states. Bands use the tolerance 0.01 nats; the replicate floors are 0.0150 (dense) and 0.0541 (terminal-only), measured at 2.5M tokens.
